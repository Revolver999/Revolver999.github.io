Hard Takeoff: Privacy Policy

Effective 31 October 2026

Hard Takeoff is a strategy game for iPhone made by Francesco Mascia ("I", "me"). This policy explains what data the game uses and why. In short: the game itself collects nothing about you. The only data collected comes from the ads shown between games, which are provided by Google.

What the game stores

Your settings, unlocks, best times and run history are saved on your iPhone only. They are never sent to me or anyone else. Deleting the app deletes them.

The game has no accounts, no sign-in and no analytics. I don't receive, sell or share any personal data.

Ads

The free game shows a full-screen ad, at most occasionally, when you start a new game. Ads never appear during play. They are served by Google AdMob, a service of Google LLC (Google Ireland Limited in the UK and EEA).

To show and measure ads, and to prevent fraud, Google may collect:

  • your device's advertising identifier (IDFA), only if you allow tracking when iOS asks;
  • your IP address and the approximate location it implies;
  • device information, such as model, operating system and language;
  • how you interact with ads, such as views and taps.

Google handles this data under its own policies: Google Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from apps that use its services.

Your choices

  • UK, EEA and Switzerland: before any ad, the game shows Google's consent form so you can choose whether your data is used for personalised ads. You can change your choice at any time in Settings → Privacy options.
  • Tracking: iOS asks whether the game may track you. If you decline, ads still appear but are not personalised. You can change this in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.
  • Personalised ads in general: iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Apple Advertising, and Google's My Ad Center.

Legal basis (UK and EU)

Personalised ads rely on your consent, which you can withdraw at any time as described above. Non-personalised ads rely on legitimate interest: keeping the game free.

Children

The game is not directed at children under 13, and ads are limited to content suitable for teenagers.

Your rights

Under the UK GDPR and EU GDPR you can ask to access, correct or delete your personal data, and you can object to its use. I hold no personal data about you, so requests about ad data are best made to Google through the links above. You can also complain to your data protection authority; in the UK this is the Information Commissioner's Office.

Changes

If the game adds a feature that uses data, such as an online leaderboard, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the date above will change.
